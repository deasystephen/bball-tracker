# Children, age and COPPA (as built)

As-built reference for the adults-only-accounts posture (#980) and its enforcement points. Keep this file current in the same PR as the code it describes. The legal analysis with its sources is a dated snapshot linked from #980; this file records what the product does.

## The posture (owner decision 2026-10-26, confirmed 2026-11-07)

Hooplings is a **general-audience** service. **Account holders must be 13 or older; 16 in the EU, the EEA, the UK and Switzerland.** A player under that age is a **roster record** that a coach or a parent/guardian enters and maintains (`User.isManaged = true`, no `workosUserId`; `docs/architecture/roster-and-invitations.md`, `docs/architecture/guardians.md`), never a login. There is no verifiable-parental-consent program and no "Kids" store category.

Why this keeps the service outside the US Children's Online Privacy Protection Rule (16 CFR Part 312, as amended with a 2026-04-22 compliance date): the Rule reaches services directed to children, and general-audience services with **actual knowledge** that they collect personal information **from** a child under 13. Information an adult enters **about** a child (a name on a roster, a photo a coach uploads) is not collected from the child (FTC COPPA FAQ A.8 and F.4). The posture holds only while nobody under 13 holds a login, so the enforcement points below exist to make that true and to undo it fast when it is not.

What is deliberately **not** stored anywhere: a date of birth, an age, or an age bracket for any person. The only age-related columns are `User.ageConfirmedAt`, the moment an account holder's answer met the minimum, and `User.ageRefusedAt`, set only when an under-minimum answer could not be acted on automatically and an operator must finish (below).

## The age screen (#981)

**Neutral, in the FTC's sense (FAQ H.3, D.7).** Free entry of the birth month (twelve pills) and year (a four-digit field), no default, no drop-down that starts at 13, no text that names a minimum on the question. A confirmation step repeats the entered month and year ("Born in March 1990?") so a typo cannot end an account; it names no age either. There is no way back: the onboarding stack has `gestureEnabled: false` for the screen, a hardware back returns to the question, and `useAuthRedirect` replaces any other route with the screen while the stored user says `ageConfirmed: false` (the sign-in paths are left to `postLoginRoute`; a deep link such as an invitation is stored as the pending return path first, so it is honoured after the answer). Push registration waits for a definite `ageConfirmed: true`, so a session persisted before the column existed collects nothing until the launch-time sync says so.

**Threshold.** `backend/src/utils/minimum-age.ts`: 13, or 16 when the device's region setting (`expo-localization` `regionCode`, sent as `regionCode` with the answer and stored nowhere) is an EU or EEA state, the UK or its dependencies, or Switzerland (`SIXTEEN_PLUS_REGIONS`). One figure for the whole GDPR area is deliberate: Article 8 lets member states set 13 to 16 and no state may set higher, the UK's statutory age is 13, and Switzerland has no fixed age, so 16 is conservative everywhere and wrong nowhere. The age is counted in whole years from the birth month, UTC, with the birth month counted as reached only once it is over (someone born in March 2013 is 13 from 1 April 2026); the day is never asked, so this is the reading under which the server never holds an account for a person it knows may still be under the minimum. A person already old enough earlier in their birth month is asked to sign up again next month; the refused sign-up is erased.

**Old enough.** `AccountService.confirmAge` sets `ageConfirmedAt` (idempotent) and logs `Age confirmed` with the user id. On the device the confirmation moment is kept (`ageConfirmedLocalAt`) so a `GET /auth/me` reply or a 403 from a request sent before the answer cannot move the flag back; `updateUser` sends no Amplitude user properties while the question is owed. The mobile store flips `user.ageConfirmed` and, for a sign-in whose analytics it held back, fires that `identifyUser` and `user_logged_in` now (`completeAgeConfirmation`; an account that existed before the column was identified at sign-in and is not counted twice), then `age_confirmed` (no properties), and the usual post-login route follows (role step, name prompt, pending deep link).

**Under the minimum.** `AccountService.refuseUnderage`, in one transaction with the row locked:

- a player account on a roster (no adult signals, `assertPlayerAccount`) is **un-claimed** back into a managed record (`unclaimManagedRow`, shared with the operator conversion): login, email, verified flag, photo (unless the team uploaded it), push and calendar tokens, pending invitations, the child's announcement replies and `ageConfirmedAt` go; the name, roster rows, events, stats and guardian links stay; `managedById` is the one head coach of the player's active-season teams, or null when none qualifies (an ADMIN can still edit the record);
- a player account on **no roster** is **deleted** through `deleteAccount` in `self` mode: erased outright when nothing references the row (a sign-up that answered and left), tombstoned otherwise;
- an account with **adult signals** (a staff role, a league admin row, guardian links, managed players, posted announcements, sent invitations, a personal league with teams: `assertPlayerAccount`, the same test the operator conversion uses) is **never undone automatically**: a wrong year on the form is far more likely than a child, and deleting it would take teams, rosters and guardian links with it. The answer is recorded as final (`User.ageRefusedAt`): every route stays 403 whatever the flag says, a second answer on the age route is refused without being read, `confirmAge` refuses, the session is revoked, the outcome is logged at error and sent to Sentry (`flow: age-refusal`, `reason: adult_account`), and an operator finishes by the runbook (`pending_operator`). The marker is cleared by the un-claim, the conversion and the deletion tombstone. Without it the live access token (verified locally against the JWKS, so a revoked session does not kill it) could answer again within its lifetime. The 403 body carries `pendingOperator: true` for this branch, and the mobile screen then says nothing has been deleted and points at `support@hooplings.com` instead of at a parent or coach (a coach who mistyped a year must not be told the service is for 13 and up).

The player and the coach who would take the record are locked `FOR UPDATE` in one id-ordered statement and the branch is read before and again under the lock, as the operator conversion does; a coach who disappears in between leaves the record with no manager rather than failing the answer. After commit the WorkOS user is deleted (`deleteIdentityOrRevokeSessions`, shared with the conversion: a 404 counts as gone; on failure every session is revoked and `identityDeleted: false` is logged), and the cleared photo's S3 object is removed. The route answers the same 403 `under_minimum_age` whatever happened, and the request logger sees only the path: the answer never reaches a log line, a column or an analytics event (tests pin all three). The mobile screen ends the session the moment the refusal arrives (`clearSession('age_refused')`, which resets the Amplitude identity, records no `user_logged_out`, and which `useAuthRedirect` does not answer with `/login`), explains the rule, and Done goes to sign-in.

**Why no device-level "cookie".** The FTC recommends a cookie so a visitor cannot back-button and retry. Here the account itself is the record: an under-age answer deletes the WorkOS user, so a retry needs a new sign-up with a new identity. A device flag was considered and rejected: on a shared family tablet it would refuse (and erase) a parent's account after a child's answer. Counsel should confirm this reading.

**Rollout (done 2026-11-07).** Existing accounts are asked once on their next launch (owner decision 2026-11-07, no backfill). The API first deployed with `AGE_GATE_ENFORCED=false` (fc16fc0), the OTA with the screen was published the same hour (group 43d913ef, runtime 1.7.0) and verified on the owner's phone, and the flag was then removed from `infra/task-definition.json` (#1019), which enforces the gate: unset means enforced, and the variable exists only to relax the gate during such a rollout. Build #34 (runtime 1.6.0) and earlier never receive the screen and have been locked out since, which is acceptable for TestFlight testers; a build #37+ phone that has not yet applied the OTA fails every request until its second launch after download.

## Analytics before the age screen

Amplitude initialises at launch, so `app_opened` and `screen_viewed` (route patterns only) fire on an anonymous device id before anyone signs in, and `sign_in_started` / `sign_in_failed` too. Nothing user-identifying is sent until `completeAgeConfirmation`. The pre-screen identifiers are persistent identifiers used only for "support for the internal operations" (16 CFR 312.2, 312.5(c)(7); FTC FAQ J.7 counts analytics of the service's own functioning), which is the Rule's own carve-out for collection before age is known; the IP-derived location stays at city level (FAQ G.3). Sentry sends no user id from the app at all.

## State app-store and age-assurance laws (#984)

Independent of COPPA, several US states route age through the stores and the operating system. Decided 2026-11-07: the in-app age screen is the enforcement at GA; Apple's **Declared Age Range API** (`com.apple.developer.declared-age-range`, a native entitlement, iOS 26+) is adopted in the first post-GA binary. Two dates bind that follow-up:

- **California AB 1043** (as amended by AB 1856), operative **2027-01-01**: a developer **must request** the OS age signal when the app is downloaded and launched, for already-installed apps by 2027-07-01, must treat it as the primary age indicator, is deemed to have actual knowledge of the bracket it receives, and may not nudge the user to change their age or share the signal. An under-13 bracket must produce the age screen's under-minimum outcome without asking the question.
- **Texas SB 2420** is in force (Fifth Circuit stay of the injunction, June 2026; Supreme Court declined to vacate, July 2026): developers assign an age rating to the app and every in-app purchase, consume the store's age and consent signal, use it only for age restrictions, compliance and safety, and delete it after use. Utah (May 2027), Louisiana (July 2027) and Alabama (January 2027) follow the same shape with their own dates.

The follow-up issue carries the build plan; `docs/release/app-store-submission.md` carries the store-metadata answers.
